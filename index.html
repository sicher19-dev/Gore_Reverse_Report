The Story

The engine failed the test the reports have been circling for two weeks. 93.8 in the first inning on a full four days of rest — no sprint to blame, no travel excuse. The five-week trend now reads 95.9 down to 93.8, and every downstream problem last night lives inside those two ticks: fastballs that used to carry over barrels got doubled, ladder attempts that used to miss by inches missed by feet, and the margin for a drifted address went to zero. Per the rule this series wrote for itself after Sacramento: this stopped being a pitch-mix conversation. It's a workload conversation.

And here is the part worth being genuinely proud of: the discipline held on the worst night of the run. Ten runs crossed and not one home run — because the crooked-number pitches were never thrown. Murakami saw exactly the two-pitch plan (double play, frozen strikeout, and a walk trying the right idea). Doyle's .677 landmine stayed welded. Vargas was conceded like the card priced. Grichuk struck out through his one door and his 108.2 EV bullet — the hardest ball of the season — found a glove on the night's biggest bullet dodged. Bad nights used to mean broken plans AND a tired arm. This was only one of those, and it's the recoverable one.

The damage anatomy: death by the middle third, at reduced speed. Eight hits, only three above 95 EV. Two doubles came directly from the heart of the plate — the middle-middle thread's fifth consecutive start — and the soft-stuff addresses drifted up with the fatigue exactly like Sacramento: a changeup at the letters, a changeup mid-zone to the whiff-machine catcher, a curve at mid-zone to the toll booth. Tired arms don't just lose velocity; they lose inches. Same lesson, louder volume.

Graded fairly, the night splits clean: seven fixable marks, and five of them are one problem. The two conceded Vargas walks were the plan working. The flare doubles from Peters and Acuña were noise. Meidroth's 101.1 EV double on an edge fastball was earned — tip the cap. What's actually on the pitcher: three mislocated soft pitches, two heart-of-plate fastballs, and two non-plan walks — every one of them the signature of an arm at 94 trying to execute a 95.5 map. The map is fine. The legs under it need September help.
