Negative run value favors the pitcher. Sixty-eight pitches, the shortest start of the season, and the least lucky-looking bad night of the series — the hits were hard and the addresses were high. The diagnosis is two-part and ordered: the plan, then the arm.

The Story

The worst process grade of the series, and it earns the label honestly: four of seven hits at 95-plus, a .734 median expected average, six fixable marks in twenty batters. This wasn't a flare night. The Rays hit executed-badly pitches hard, exactly the way a flat-swing contact lineup is supposed to when it gets fastballs at 66% and soft stuff at the wrong height. The Rangers won anyway; the ledger doesn't grade the scoreboard.

Two things broke, in order: the plan, then the arm. The plan first — the scout's opening page said soft-first at 94.5 against a team with no ladder holes, and the afternoon went two curveballs and forty-five fastballs. Whether it was the day-game routine, the temptation of quick outs against contact hitters, or simple drift, the September blueprint that carried a tired arm past the A's five days earlier didn't show up. Then the arm — two innings at the setting, then 93.6, then 93.2 with the ride at 15.6 inches. The fourth-inning hits (Yandy 105.0 on a hung curve, Aranda 98.3 on a sinker up) are what a 93-mph arm executing a 95-mph map looks like. Chicago, again, a tick lower.

The height ledger is the whole coaching note. Sinkers to green-list hitters at 2.86 and 3.04 feet — the whitelist did its job on names and the pitch did the opposite of its job on height; sink at the letters is a line-drive machine, and both were. A changeup at 2.76 feet — Serven, Doyle, and now Fortes have the same receipt. A changeup that caught the bottom of the zone to DeLuca, whose entire door is below it. A curve at mid-zone to the flattest swing in the series. None of these required a different pitch selection to fix; every one of them needed six inches. The addresses are the assignment, same as they've been since Sacramento — and at 93 they're the whole game.

What survived, and it matters: nothing left the yard, and the plans that were followed worked. Caminero — the fastest bat in nine reports — went hitless, conceded twice as priced. Vilade struck out on the only heat-first plan on the card. The retired middle-middle thread stayed retired. Zero cutters. Four straight homerless starts, twenty-five innings, against four lineups with real power: the discipline that keeps crooked numbers off the board is still intact. The problem Sunday wasn't the bans. It was everything between them — the sequencing default and the vertical inches — and both of those are downstream of an arm that has now told the calendar the same thing three times.

Plan Adherence

Graded against the Rays II scout — the first one written for the 94.5 setting, and the first one whose setting rule was ignored.

FAILThe setting rule — soft-first at 94.5 — was inverted: 66% fastballs, a season high, and two curveballs after twenty percent last start. The scout's first page said the fastball is a location pitch against this flat-swing lineup, not the plan. The afternoon ran the opposite: heat-first with a fading heater to the one team built to put it in play. The 9/1 blueprint was proven five days earlier; it didn't make the trip to Sunday.
FAILThe arm: 94.8 and 94.6 for two innings, then 93.6 and 93.2 — the lowest reading of the season — with the ride down to 15.6 inches. The setting held for eight batters and then the game outlasted it. This is past the extra-day conversation: in-game decay after 40 pitches is the workload story at its end-state. The rest question is now a skip-a-turn question.
MISSHeight discipline: five of the six fixable marks are pitches thrown too high. Two sinkers at the top of the zone (Simpson 96.3, Aranda 98.3 — the whitelist held on WHO, failed on WHERE), a changeup at the letters (Fortes — the standing rule's third receipt), a changeup that caught the zone to a chase-only hitter (DeLuca 106.8), a curve at mid-zone to Yandy (105.0). Same sentence as Chicago: tired arms lose inches. This time the inches were all vertical.
PASSWhat held: the Caminero and Vilade plans, the retired thread, the cutter, the whitelist names, the homer. Caminero: two walks (one intentional), zero hits, exactly as priced. Vilade: ten fastballs, a strikeout, the only heat-first at-bat on the card executed as drawn (the walk was misses, not menu). Zero middle-middle contact — the retired thread stays retired. Zero cutters. Four sinkers, all to green-list names (seventh straight clean whitelist by name). And zero home runs — four straight homerless starts, 25 innings, through four lineups with power.
MISSChangeup 26% vs righties, three of twelve above 2.6 feet; the slider returned (five, 20% to lefties) with a completely different shape — 0.3 inches of ride, a sweeper, not the eraser. Share was close; height was the leak. The slider's shape drift joins the ride decay as a stuff-tracking item, not a plan item.
PASSThe walk thread improved: three walks, two of them priced to Caminero. Vilade's five-pitch pass at eye level was the only unpriced free base — and even that was the right menu executed badly. First-pitch strikes at 55% and a 43% zone rate are the tell that the count discipline still isn't where September needs it, but the free bases stopped going to hitters with no book.
