The Story

Zero strikeouts against a whiff lineup, and the explanation is one number: 25% first-pitch strikes. The scout drew nine strikeouts through doors above the zone (six hitters over 33% elevated) and below it (six over 55%). Both doors require a two-strike count to open, and the night never got there — twelve of sixteen at-bats started 1-0, three walks came on menus the cards never drew, and Boston, which chases at 44% when it has to, never had to. One whiff on twenty-seven swings isn't a stuff story. It's a count story, and the count thread that improved in Arizona regressed to its worst reading of the season.

The homer was the most predictable pitch of the night, and the cards had already banned it three different ways. Contreras, 2-0, a slider at px −0.01 and 2.71 feet — dead center. His card said slider off the plate, never in the zone; it said the walk was priced at 23 free passes; it said the vertical extremes were the whole plan. A 2-0 count to the fastest bat on the roster is the moment the bans matter most, and the bans were suspended for exactly one pitch. 105.7, three runs, and a 29-inning homerless streak gone. The rule grows a clause tonight: hitters' counts get no banned pitches.

The blueprint is alternating: held against the A's, inverted against the Rays, held in Arizona, inverted against Boston. 58% fastballs and a 9% changeup share — the identity pitch at its lowest usage of the series — against a lineup with six changeup customers over 50% chase or whiff. The gamebar rule was written for this exact failure and it still didn't make it to the mound. That's not a scouting problem anymore; it's a pre-game alignment problem, and it belongs in the pitcher-catcher meeting, not on page one of a report.

What held, and the one honest positive: the ride is back. Two hits in four innings; eleven clean outs; Abreu's two bullets (94.8, 99.7) on zone heat found gloves; Anthony got the slider plan and a groundout; Jones, Rutschman and Eaton were retired on weak flies. And the four-seam's ride came back to 16.5 inches — the June number — even as the gas hit 92.8 in the fifth. The shape isn't decaying; the tank is emptying. Eleven days left in the season, and the last two watch lists have said the same thing about the calendar.
